\section{Introduction}

A library of papyrus scrolls was carbonized at Herculaneum during the AD~79 eruption of Mount Vesuvius. The rolls survive, but they cannot be opened: the papyrus has become fragile carbon, and the writing is trapped inside a tightly wound coil. Synchrotron micro-CT records the internal geometry without touching the object, and the text becomes readable only after the correct papyrus surface has been located in three dimensions and \emph{virtually unwrapped} to a plane.

From the perspective of geometry processing, the computational object virtual unwrapping requires is unusual. Scroll CT data does not represent the boundary of a solid; instead, it is a single open sheet, many meters long, rolled into a hundred or more turns that in places touch, fuse, delaminate, and tear. A useful reconstruction has to keep neighbouring turns distinct, distinguish a gap in the evidence from a real hole in the papyrus, and preserve physical length so that fibres and writing stay continuous after flattening. Getting one of those wrong does not produce a slightly worse page; it produces a confidently wrong one.

Automatic U-Net segmenters~\cite{ronneberger2015u} supply strong voxel evidence but not that object. Their output can be several voxels thick, discontinuous across dropouts, or fused across adjacent wraps where the sheets are pressed together. Classical surface reconstruction is also not suited to this data: marching cubes~\cite{lorensen1998marching} and Poisson reconstruction~\cite{kazhdan2006poisson} are built to recover a closed, two-sided boundary, while a scroll needs a single-sided open manifold. Nor can parameterization rescue the result afterwards. A flattener minimizes distortion against whatever mesh it is given~\cite{sheffer2005abf++}, so two wraps fused into one surface flatten into one clean page; and the freedom an atlas packer relies on --- place charts wherever empty space allows~\cite{limper2018box} --- does not exist here, because horizontal position on the page \emph{is} the answer: developed arc length, shared by every disconnected fragment of a wrap, and fixed only up to a whole number of turns that no continuous relaxation can recover. Worse, the failures at all three stages are often invisible when rendered: a short connection that happens to walk a full turn, a chart placed on the wrong wrap, or a fabricated continuation through a dark region of the CT all look like clean geometry. They are only detectable by asking what evidence supports them.

{\em ScrollFiesta} is a meshing and unwrapping toolchain built for the Herculaneum papyri. It takes a coarse surface-probability volume, the matching CT, and an estimate of the scroll axis, and works in two domains (Fig.~\ref{fig:dataflow}).

The organizing idea is a single geometric quantity. Because the object is a spiral, the branch-cut-free winding coordinate of Equation~\eqref{eq:winding} --- how many turns' worth of radius a point has climbed, minus how far around the roll it has travelled --- is constant along one wrap and increases by exactly one on stepping outward to the next. It is therefore the natural coordinate in which ``same sheet'' and ``next sheet'' differ by a unit, and it is precisely the quantity that edge length, aspect ratio, manifoldness, and self-intersection cannot measure. Every stage below carries it: it guards a pivot in ball pivoting, defines the relation graph between sheet tracks, becomes the label set of a Markov random field, and finally becomes the horizontal axis of the flattened page.

Its input is a completed surface-probability volume. Repairing that volume --- distilling fine-resolution evidence into the coarse predictor, and closing short dropouts in the traced sheet --- is the subject of the companion \emph{Socratic Method} report~\cite{socraticmethod}, and everything below assumes it has already been done. ScrollFiesta itself adds and removes no probability mass; it decides what surface the volume it is handed describes.

\emph{First}, in the identity domain (Section~\ref{sec:sheetbooks}), the prediction is traced into material tracks and lifted into single-valued sheets. Signed radial contacts between coexisting tracks give integer winding relations, which compose through a maximum-support forest repaired by integer subtree shifts --- an identity structure that is deliberately not mesh connectivity, since two pieces of one sheet across a hole are disconnected and two different wraps joined by one fusion are connected.

\emph{Second}, in the metric domain (Sections~\ref{sec:percube}--\ref{sec:finishing}), local geometry is extracted cube by cube as single-sided open meshes; a two-sided generalized winding number field and local component relations are reconciled as uncertain factors in an integer Markov random field; and the resulting geometry is fitted to a ribbon of quadrilaterals whose horizontal coordinate is exact developed arc length.

\paragraph{Contributions.} Relative to the July~31 public release, this update contributes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item an identity structure that is deliberately \emph{not} mesh connectivity: sheet tracks carrying pairwise integer winding relations, composed through a maximum-support forest with integer subtree-shift loop closure, so that two fragments of one sheet separated by a hole remain one identity while two wraps joined by a single fusion do not become one;
  \item an inference formulation for winding that replaces hard rounding of one fragile measurement: a two-sided generalized winding number field yields robust per-component unaries, local continuation and radial-order observations become pairwise factors, and alpha--beta swap graph cuts solve the resulting integer-label MRF over residual turn offsets; and
  \item a metric quad ribbon whose horizontal coordinate is exact developed arc length rather than the output of a distortion-minimizing parameterization --- fitted against one shared metric-rate map, reconciled by Huber-IRLS sparse least squares, untangled by exact triangle-contact response, and composited against held-out CT.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope.} This is a progress report, not a claim that a Herculaneum scroll has been read end to end. The winding MRF improves its objective on a fresh $4\times5\times5$ mesh, but the sparse conflict penalties layered on top of it do not yet reduce duplicate claims without damaging the relation evidence they are built from. The sheet-track ladder runs through $21\times21\times21$ cubes; the metric ribbon is measured at $4\times5\times5$, and the larger metric numbers come from separately frozen upstream states rather than one controlled scaling curve. Each such limit is stated with the result it belongs to.

\paragraph{How to read this paper.} Section~\ref{sec:background} is a two-way glossary, covering both geometric mesh processing and the scrolls themselves, and should be read first by anybody who is not familiar with one half of the problem. A reader arriving from geometry processing who has never handled a scroll wants Sections~\ref{sec:physical} and~\ref{sec:data}; a reader who knows the scrolls but not the reconstruction literature wants Section~\ref{sec:gp}, which states exactly which standard assumptions this object violates. Section~\ref{sec:overview} gives the data flow stage by stage. Sections~\ref{sec:percube} to \ref{sec:finishing} then take those stages in order, each opening with a plain statement of what the stage does physically before the technical account begins. Section~\ref{sec:results} collects the measurements, including the negative results. Appendix~\ref{app:developments} is the only part of the paper organized by date: it records what changed since the July 31, 2026 public release.
