\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Virtual unwrapping of the Herculaneum scrolls.} Virtual unwrapping was introduced by Seales et al.~\shortcite{seales2016damage}, who recovered text from the En-Gedi scroll by segmenting its surface within a CT volume and flattening it to a plane. Applying the same idea to the far more tightly wound Herculaneum scrolls has driven several automatic approaches collected by the Vesuvius Challenge. \emph{Spiral fitting}~\cite{henderson2026virtually} uses the fact that the scroll was originally one rolled sheet and fits an extruded spiral to the prediction by solving a variational problem; it degrades where a noisy or locally collapsed prediction cannot support a single spiral. \emph{Surface tracing} grows local surface patches and must subsequently decide which fronts or patches belong together. \emph{ThaumatoAnakalyptor} extracts a point cloud through image filtering, classifies points into wraps, and reconstructs a surface through winding-angle assignment.

These families expose the same underlying trade. A strong global scroll prior can cross damaged regions but imposes an idealization the object does not obey; purely local evidence represents folds and tears faithfully but leaves a difficult composition problem. ScrollFiesta uses both, and its contribution is largely about \emph{where} each decision is made rather than about a new primitive. Local geometry is reconstructed per cube. But large-scale identity is represented as sheet tracks carrying an explicit winding relation --- never inferred from whether two triangle soups happen to be weldable --- and the developed ribbon then consumes those identities and the measured phase field. This separation is what lets the system leave a global continuation open while still placing the local material on either side of it.

\paragraph{Generalized winding fields and discrete labeling.} Generalized winding numbers extend the solid-angle winding function to imperfect oriented triangle meshes~\cite{jacobson2013winding}; hierarchical approximations make them practical on triangle soups~\cite{barill2018fast}, while a recent boundary-edge reduction provides an exact alternative~\cite{xie2026fast}. Those works use the field primarily for inside--outside reasoning. We instead sample both sides of an open papyrus sheet: the mean of the two values is a noisy global turn cue, while their difference should be one and acts as a local reliability measurement. We do not round this cue into a label. It becomes a robust unary in an integer Markov random field whose pairwise factors retain every independently measured continuation and radial-order relation. Alpha--beta swap graph cuts~\cite{boykov2001fast} optimize small residual label windows hierarchically; sparse exclusion penalties represent evidence that one particular component--turn assignment is impossible without pretending that a new Gaussian observation was made.

\paragraph{Surface reconstruction and meshing.} Surface reconstruction is a mature field, but its standard tools are a poor fit here because they are designed to produce \emph{two-sided, watertight} surfaces. Marching cubes~\cite{lorensen1998marching} and its descendants, such as dual contouring~\cite{ju2002dual} and differentiable FlexiCubes~\cite{shen2023flexicubes}, extract a closed isosurface. Poisson reconstruction~\cite{kazhdan2006poisson} fits a closed indicator function and, in distributed form~\cite{kazhdan2023distributed}, scales to very large volumes. A scroll sheet is instead open and single-sided, so ScrollFiesta combines a moving-least-squares collapse with guarded ball pivoting~\cite{bernardini1999ball} and explicit topology repair (Section~\ref{sec:percube}). The choice is not incidental: a closed-surface method has no vocabulary in which to leave two nearby sheets unconnected, which is precisely the configuration this data is full of.

\paragraph{Mesh parameterization and atlas construction.} Flattening a surface is likewise mature, but classical objectives solve a different problem. Angle-based flattening and LSCM~\cite{sheffer2005abf++} minimize angular or conformal distortion without providing the global layout a long, damaged scroll requires. For a clean disk-like patch ScrollFiesta delegates to a symmetric-Dirichlet flattener~\cite{smith2015bijective}, chosen because it is locally injective by construction: on a long, high-aspect, damaged patch the failure that matters is a folded chart, not a few degrees of angular error. For a whole scroll, per-chart injectivity is only the beginning, for the two reasons given in Section~\ref{sec:coords}.

StrokeStrip~\cite{pagurek2021strokestrip} is the closest local parameterization model, in that it fits one common coordinate system to a cluster of strokes rather than to a single connected patch; ScrollFiesta adopts its slice-domain view. An earlier version of this system paired that view with a tabu search~\cite{glover1989tabu} over integer chart depths, which removed 99.5\% of exact UV overlaps but left 41 usable weld relations torn --- a result we retain as a reproducible baseline (Appendix~\ref{app:tabu}) and discuss in Section~\ref{sec:deadends}. The current method keeps the slice-domain metric observations but replaces search over chart depths with inference over turn labels: a maximum-support forest initializes the frame, loop closure and the MRF reconsider every retained relation, and unresolved sites carry a confidence rather than a fabricated certainty. Robust sparse least squares then closes metric residuals; it does not choose which wrap a component belongs to. Exact 3-D collision response, CT attachment, and layer compositing are separate operators rather than additional terms in one atlas objective, precisely so that a low-overlap score cannot buy a wrong physical identity.
