\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The September release makes the native sheet workflow available through one
generic configuration while retaining the public C API. The reference
$4\times5\times5$ check produces the partial CT-textured sheet in
Figure~\ref{fig:public-release-sheet}: 47.4\% source-area coverage,
171 overlapping triangle pairs, and unchanged represented source geometry.
It remains unqualified. The research conclusions below concern the distinct
August campaigns and do not imply that the public defaults reproduce them.

The recurring difficulty in virtual unwrapping is that the decisive error --- a surface that has walked onto the neighbouring turn --- is invisible to every measure a mesh can supply. ScrollFiesta's response is to carry the quantity that \emph{can} see it. A branch-cut-free winding coordinate, constant along one wrap and incremented by one at each step outward, is computed alongside the geometry and used as the decision variable at every scale: as a radial bound on a voxel-domain bridge, as a growth guard on a pivoting front, as the integer relation between two sheet tracks, as the label set of a Markov random field, and finally as the horizontal axis of the flattened page.

The three contributions of this round follow from that. First, identity is represented apart from geometry: sheet tracks carry pairwise integer winding relations, composed through a maximum-support forest with integer subtree-shift loop closure, so a remeshing or welding decision cannot quietly change which turn a piece of papyrus belongs to. Second, the metric ribbon \emph{consumes} that turn assignment rather than searching for one, so a low-distortion or low-overlap solution cannot buy itself a different physical identity. Third, winding is inferred instead of rounded: a two-sided generalized winding number field supplies robust per-component unaries, local continuation and radial-order observations supply pairwise factors, and alpha--beta swap graph cuts solve the resulting integer MRF.

The representation scales. Sheet books carry more than 70\% of evidenced material at every rung through $21\times21\times21$, and the largest winding assembly orders 15,457 tracks across 112 winds while retaining 99.3\% of assigned material. The metric lane then shows that a solid, readable quad ribbon can be obtained without letting an atlas search own physical identity: exact arclength against one shared metric-rate map establishes placement, Huber-IRLS sparse least squares closes measured cracks and contacts, an exact contact response untangles the coil, and CT attachment moves vertices onto the papyrus band while keeping interpolated material labelled apart from measured material. On the strongest $4\times5\times5$ campaigns, all 4.033 million fixed-topology faces are conserved, every marked missing region is recovered from a supported layer, and the attachment loop reaches a zero-coverage-fault fixed point at Sander $p95/p99$ 1.190/1.380.

The probabilistic winding result resolves the long-standing ambiguity more directly than a smooth atlas can, because it puts the integer where the ambiguity actually lives. On the fresh $4\times5\times5$ pile, the clean-sheet field jump holds at 99.1\% of queried vertices; the MRF changes four component labels, lowers its energy from 221.369 to 200.486, and retains 0.99971/0.99816 continuation/order satisfaction. The failed penalty experiments are equally informative: broad variants halve the duplicate-claim cells only by damaging the relations that produced the labels, and the targeted one-bin rule clears its two four-claimant cells at more than fivefold the energy. A count of spatial collisions is a weaker signal than a measured relation, and the experiments say so numerically.

The negative results are part of the contribution rather than an appendix to it. No conflict-penalty variant improves both of its objectives; a slab splitter separates 0 of 246 fused runs despite a convincing visualization; a tabu atlas removes 99.5\% of UV overlaps while tearing 41 weld relations; and generated $10\times10\times10$ continuation crosses wraps its source had already fused. Read together they locate the remaining failure precisely: missing material and large-scale wrap identity must be settled before geometry is filled or flattened, because no downstream optimizer can recover an identity that is absent from its input.

\paragraph{Limitations.} This is not a complete automatic unwrapping of a Herculaneum scroll. The August one-command quad-ribbon campaign completed its canonical $4\times5\times5$ run from the pre-weld pile, but the larger metric results come from frozen inputs of earlier lineages and are not an end-to-end scaling curve. The $21^3$ sheet book supplies radial order but no comparable measurement for longitudinal continuation across a large hole --- which is why Fig.~\ref{fig:dataflow} draws that link as absent. The elastic shell retains 4,073 post-snap contacts on the full sheet. The crushed inner core has no measurable pitch, so the winding coordinate is undefined there and the region is excluded regardless of how it renders. The targeted conflict penalties have not resolved their two cells without sacrificing stronger continuation and order evidence.

\paragraph{Next experiments.} The remaining work follows directly from those limits: obtain an independent measurement of absolute turn error, which the MRF currently cannot supply for itself; redesign the conflict penalties so they reduce remote duplicate claims without expanding span or lowering relation satisfaction; carry the global fit forward from the $21\times21\times21$ pre-weld pile; and feed same-wrap continuation relations into the ribbon fit without merging components --- the one link Fig.~\ref{fig:dataflow} still draws as absent.

ScrollFiesta is not yet a read scroll. What it offers is a formulation in which the question that decides the answer --- which turn of the roll a piece of papyrus belongs to --- is represented explicitly, measured where it is still observable, and never delegated to an optimizer that cannot see it.
